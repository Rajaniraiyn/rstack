# Structures to break

Each pattern below is a predictable AI shape. The fix keeps the meaning and drops the shape.

## Binary contrast

"It's not X, it's Y." / "The question isn't X, the question is Y." State Y directly.

Before: "The question isn't the model. It's the eval."
After: "The eval matters more than the model."

## Negative listing

"Not a X. Not a Y. A Z." Builds false suspense before revealing the point. Just say Z.

Before: "Not a bug. Not a feature. A fundamental design flaw."
After: "A fundamental design flaw."

## "Not just X but Y"

"It's not just X, it's Y." State the point directly.

Before: "It's not just a faster build, it's a more reliable one."
After: "The build is faster and more reliable."

## Rule of three

Forcing ideas into groups of three. Use the natural number. One tricolon can be fine; stacked tricolons are a pattern recognition failure.

Before: "Simple to install, simple to configure, simple to maintain."
After: "Simple to install and configure."

## Synonym cycling

Rotating synonyms for the same thing to avoid repeating it.

Before: "The agent reviews the draft. The assistant scores the piece. The tool suggests fixes."
After: "The agent reviews the draft, scores it, and suggests fixes."

## False range

"from X to Y" where X and Y aren't on a meaningful scale.

Before: "From innovation to implementation to cultural transformation."
After: "It covers innovation and implementation."

## Self-posed rhetorical question

"The result? Devastating." / "The worst part? Nobody saw it coming." Ask and answer in a statement.

Before: "The worst part? The eval hangs."
After: "The eval hangs."

## Dramatic fragmentation

"X. And Y. And Z." / "That's it. That's the whole thing." Stacked fragments for manufactured emphasis. Use complete sentences.

Before: "Speed. That's it. That's the tradeoff."
After: "The tradeoff is speed."

## Anaphora abuse

Repeating the same sentence opening three or more times.

Before: "They assume users will pay. They assume developers will build. They assume ecosystems will emerge."
After: "They assume users will pay, developers will build, and ecosystems will emerge."

## Listicle in a trench coat

"The first wall... The second wall... The third wall..." Numbered or labeled points dressed up as prose. Write paragraphs.

Before: "The first wall is the absence of a free API. The second wall is the lack of delegated access."
After: "A free API is missing, as is delegated access."

## Colon reveal

A noun phrase, a colon, then a lowercase dramatic reveal.

Before: "The best part: it learns."
After: "It learns, which is what makes it work."

## Faux-insight setup

"This is the part most people skip." / "Here's what nobody tells you." Flatters the writer as the lone expert. Make the claim stand on its own.

Before: "The part everyone misses: distribution is the real moat."
After: "Distribution is the moat."

## Fractal summary

Telling the reader what you are about to say, saying it, then summarizing what you said. Applied at every level this pads the piece.

Before: "In this section we'll explore the tradeoffs. [three paragraphs] As we've seen, the tradeoffs are real."
After: Say it once, concretely.

## Summary-recap ending

"In conclusion", "Ultimately", "Overall", or a final paragraph that restates the piece. End on the last concrete point, takeaway, or next action.

Before: "In conclusion, the future of reliable builds depends on testing."
After: "Test on the commit, not the branch."

## Fake-profound kicker

The final "deep" line that turns the point into a metaphor or mic-drop. Delete it, and don't rewrite it into a better metaphor. End on the clearest concrete sentence already in the draft.

Before: "...and that is the quiet revolution. The reinvention of the humble build."
After: "The build now fails on a column rename, before anything ships."

## Punctuation tells

- Em dashes: avoid entirely. Use periods or commas only. No parentheses, no en dashes, no hyphen-as-dash.
- Bold-first bullets: every item starting with a bolded keyword. Remove the bold leads.
- Unicode arrows and curly quotes: use straight quotes and plain text.
- Title case headings: use sentence case.
- Decorative emojis: remove from headings and bullets.

## Robotic rhythm

Repeated sentence shapes, identical paragraph structures, stacked punchy fragments. Vary the shape only when it helps the point. Two items beat three. End paragraphs differently.
